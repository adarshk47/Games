Winter health: stay well when it is cold 🧣

Simple Ayurvedic habits and food for the cold months – so you feel warm, strong and active all winter.

DGVaidya Dolly GuptaGeneral Physician (Ayurveda) · 5 min read

Why winter needs a little extra care

In Ayurveda, early and late winter are called Hemant and Shishir ritu. The cold and dry air can make skin, joints and breathing uncomfortable. The good news: digestion (agni) is usually stronger in winter, so the body can take warm, nourishing food – it just needs the right routine.

🌅 Your winter daily routine

☀️Wake with the lightStart the day with a glass of warm water.
💆Oil massage (abhyanga)Warm sesame or mustard oil before your bath keeps skin and joints happy.
🚿Lukewarm, not very hot bathVery hot water dries the skin even more.
🌞15–20 minutes of sunMorning sunlight warms the body and lifts the mood.
🚶Move every dayA brisk walk, yoga or surya namaskar – keep the body warm from inside.
🧦Cover head, ears and feetMost body heat is lost from these. Moisturise lips and skin.

🥘 What to eat in winter

Warm, freshly cooked mealsGajar, palak, methi, sarson, mooliShakarkandi (sweet potato) Bajra, makka, jowar rotiTil and gud – a littleA spoon of ghee Soups and dalAmla, oranges, guavaAdrak, lahsun, haldi in cookingWarm haldi milk at night

🚫 Avoid or cut down

Cold drinks and ice creamStale or fridge-cold foodToo much tea and coffee Heavy, late dinnersSkipping mealsSitting all day

👵 Extra care for some people

Elderly people and anyone with BP, heart disease, asthma or diabetes should keep warm, not skip their medicines, and keep sweets (even til-gud) in check. Ask your doctor before changing anything.

See a doctor if you have:
  • Fever for more than 3 days
  • Breathlessness or wheezing
  • Chest pain – call 112 at once
  • A cough that does not go away in 2 weeks

This is general information, not personal medical advice. Never stop or change a medicine without asking your doctor.
